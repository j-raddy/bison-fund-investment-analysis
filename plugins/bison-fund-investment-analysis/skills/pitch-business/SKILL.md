# Pitch Business

Section 01 of five. Slides 01 to 10. Core question: what is this business, and is its market growing?

## Triggers

- The hub reaches Phase 4, Business, the first section in the fixed order.
- A request to explain the business, its segments, its operating metrics, or its market size.

## Inputs

- The grounding digests, read before any thesis existed.
- The analyst's Phase 2 answers.
- Filings, earnings transcripts, and company investor materials.

## Required elements

- A one-line company description and headline scale metrics (revenue, customers, assets).
- Products and services, segment by segment, with each segment's revenue contribution where disclosed.
- The operating metrics the market actually trades on (users, net deposits, assets under custody).
- Industry outlook and the structural drivers behind it.
- Market opportunity: total and serviceable addressable market sizing, with a cited source and a
  stated growth rate.
- Geographic and product footprint.

Detail and the worked ordering: `references/business.md`.

## Analyst owns

Why this business is durable, not just what it sells. The assistant assembles the record; the
durability claim is the analyst's.

## Evidence

The tag shape is in `sourcing-standard`. Market sizing is an estimate and is labeled as one. The
section's Key takeaway is the analyst's take on what the record says, never an opinion floating free
of it.

## Output

A sourced Business section draft, mapped to slides 01 to 10, ending with the list of judgment calls
the analyst still owns.

## Pitfalls

- **A market size with no source or no growth rate.** Both are required; one without the other is not
  usable on a slide.
- **Segment revenue that does not add up** because one segment's contribution was disclosed and
  another's was inferred. Say which is which.
- **Narrating the business instead of reporting it.** Adjective stacks about the opportunity are the
  failure mode this section invites.
