# Socratic Question Bank

These questions exist so the analyst thinks before the assistant drafts. Ask the whole
set, then **wait**. Do not answer on the analyst's behalf and do not begin research until
they have written a first-pass belief for each. Short, honest answers are fine, the point
is to make the analyst's current thinking explicit so the research can test it.

## Phase 1, Intake questions

1. **In one sentence, what does this company do?** (Their words, not the company's boilerplate.)
2. **Where does the money actually come from?** Name the main revenue lines and roughly
  what share each is.
3. **Who is the customer, and why do they stay?**
4. **What do you think the market misunderstands about this company**, why might the
  stock be mispriced?
5. **What would have to be true for you to be right?** (State the 2–3 assumptions the
  thesis rests on.)
6. **What would make you wrong?** (Name the single strongest counter-case in advance.)
7. **What is your current prior, buy, hold, or sell, and how confident are you (1–5)?**

Record the analyst's answers. They become the test the evidence base has to pass.

## Phase 4, Red-team questions

Before the bull case is final, put these to the analyst and argue the bear side yourself:

1. **Which assumption is doing the most work** in the thesis, and how sensitive is the
  valuation to it?
2. **What is the strongest version of the short case?** Argue it in good faith.
3. **What does the market already know** that you are treating as your edge?
4. **What single data point, if it came in differently, would break the thesis?**
5. **For each named risk: probability (low/med/high) × impact (low/med/high)?** Place it
  on the matrix, then decide which risks are real and which are noise.

## Ground rules for the assistant

- One question set at a time. Don't bury the analyst under twenty questions at once.
- If the analyst answers "I don't know," that is a green light to *research that specific
 question together*, not a green light to answer everything else.
- Never treat the assistant's own answer as if the analyst said it.
