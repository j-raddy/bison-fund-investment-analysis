# Pitch Competition

Section 02 of five. Slides 11 to 18. Core question: who does it compete with, and who runs it?

## Triggers

- The hub reaches Phase 4, Competition, after Business.
- A request for a peer comparison, a moat argument, the regulatory picture, or a management read.

## Inputs

- The Business section, already drafted, so the peer table uses the same metrics.
- Each peer's own filings, for metrics on a consistent basis.
- The grounding digests, for peer and news context.

## Required elements

- A peer comparison table on consistent metrics: net revenue, growth, net income, margin, accounts,
  client assets, revenue per account, market capitalization, price to earnings.
- Revenue diversification: how the mix shifts across periods, and what the shift means.
- The regulatory landscape that shapes the business model.
- The competitive moat, evidenced. Recurring subscription revenue against volume-dependent revenue is
  the kind of distinction that carries evidence.
- Recent M&A and the rationale for each deal.
- Management team: tenure, background, and executive compensation against peers.

Detail: `references/competition.md`.

## Analyst owns

Whether the moat is real, and whether management is aligned. The assistant lays out the comparison; the
judgment is the analyst's.

## Evidence

The tag shape is in `sourcing-standard`. Every peer metric comes from that peer's own filing or an
established data provider, dated.

## Output

A sourced Competition section draft, mapped to slides 11 to 18, ending with the judgment calls the
analyst still owns.

## Pitfalls

- **A peer table on inconsistent bases.** Mixing a GAAP margin with a non-GAAP margin, or a fiscal
  year with a calendar year, invalidates the comparison. State the basis per column.
- **A moat asserted, not evidenced.** "Strong brand" is not evidence. A recurring revenue share is.
- **Compensation comparisons without the metric named.** Total compensation, realized pay, and grant
  value are different numbers; say which one the table uses.
