# Worked Example, The Robinhood (HOOD) Pitch

A completed Bison Fund pitch exists as the reference standard: **Robinhood Markets
(NASDAQ: HOOD), dated September 8, 2026**, a ~40-slide equity pitch. Use it to show
analysts what a finished pitch looks like, not as a template to copy. (No reusable
HOOD template ships with this skill, on purpose: the next name gets its own work.)

**The HOOD deck file is not distributed with this skill; this is a written description.** For the
built deck bar that does ship, use the `deck-build` skill: its standard states the bar slide by slide,
so no example deck is needed to build or judge one.

## What the deck does well, point analysts at these

These are the moves that make a pitch credible, and each maps to a phase in the loop:

- **A diversification insight, not just numbers.** Robinhood's crypto revenue share fell
 from ~30% to ~13% of transaction revenue, yet the segment still grew ~44%, event
 contracts more than covered the shortfall, while a crypto-only peer printed a loss on
 the same tape. That is a Phase 3 Competition insight: the mix shift *is* the story.
- **A framing that forces a decision.** Robinhood's average account is ~1/25th the size of
 a large broker's, monetizing at a fraction of the revenue per account. Closing that gap
 is the bull case; failing to is the bear case. The deck states both. That is a Phase 4
 red-team move.
- **A moat argued from evidence.** Recurring subscription revenue (Gold) set against
 volume-dependent trading revenue, the direct answer to "this is just a volatility play."
- **Every claim dated and sourced**, with footnotes stating the basis of each comparison.

## What the deck leaves blank, and why that is the lesson

The deck deliberately leaves the analyst's judgment calls open:
- Price target and implied upside read "to be completed."
- The three thesis pillars read "Headline to be defined / Supporting line to be written."

**That is exactly the point of this skill.** The research scaffolding can be filled in fast
with an assistant; the price target, the pillars, and the recommendation cannot be, and
must not be. Show analysts the blanks and ask them to fill them, in their own words.

## Where the deck is a cautionary example

The reference deck ships with small defects, a few typos and some duplicated slide numbers.
Show them: even a strong pitch can carry proofreading slips. The Phase 5 verification pass
(run a final proofread) is where those get caught. Quality is part of the work.

## How to use it in a session

1. Show the deck early, in Phase 0 or 1, so analysts know the target output.
2. Return to specific slides as the matching section is drafted.
3. Use the blank price target and pillars as the moment to hand the pen back to the analyst.
4. In Phase 5, have the analyst find the typos themselves, it teaches the habit.
